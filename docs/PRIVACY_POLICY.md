# Privacy Policy

**Application:** MineDock 0.5.0 desktop application. **Technical audit:** 10 October 2026. **Notice updated:** 11 October 2026.

MineDock is a local Minecraft server manager maintained by [Bobydeluxe](https://github.com/Bobydeluxe). Its official home and source are [the GitHub repository](https://github.com/Bobydeluxe/MineDock); packages are distributed through [GitHub Releases](https://github.com/Bobydeluxe/MineDock/releases). This notice describes the desktop application, its online features and information deliberately sent to the maintainer. It is based on the [verified source-code audit](privacy-audit.md), not a packet capture of every dependency or program started by MineDock.

Privacy contact: **[minedockapp@gmail.com](mailto:minedockapp@gmail.com)**. Do not send passwords, full player databases, private worlds or identity documents without an agreed secure process. The application's technical behavior is documented below; unresolved correspondence arrangements are explicitly identified in [Contact and your rights](#contact-and-your-rights).

## Responsibility and scope

The server operator controls the player information on their computer/server, including applicable notices, access, legal basis and retention. Installing MineDock does not send those local files to its developer. Minecraft servers, Java/PHP, loaders, mods, plugins, local map services, antivirus and the operating system are separate programs with their own network behavior and policies. Review their settings separately.

GitHub and the API/download providers receive the specific requests listed below. Their processing is governed by their own notices; a provider is not automatically the MineDock maintainer's processor. Ownership/EULA confirmation for game resources is not blanket consent to personal-data processing.

## Data stored on your computer

MineDock stores the following in its user-data directory and the server/backup folders you select:

- Server profiles, paths, engine/game versions, configuration, preferences and scheduled tasks.
- A local SQLite database (`app.db`), including Minecraft usernames/UUIDs, player observations and sessions, administration records and private operator notes.
- Native world, inventory, statistics, whitelist, operator and ban files, which can contain personal information.
- Backups, restore previews, inventory safety copies, configuration history, database migration copies and recovery trash.
- Application/error logs, local crash evidence, console output and process/player-count performance measurements.
- Protected RCON credentials, cached catalogs/icons/skins, private verified game resources, selected mod/pack resources and downloaded application updates.

The data root is `MINEDOCK_DATA_DIR` when configured, otherwise the Electron user-data directory plus `data`. Server and backup roots can be chosen separately. MineDock implements no automatic upload of SQLite, worlds, inventories, private notes or backups. A shared folder, OS cloud backup, plugin or deliberate export can copy them elsewhere independently. Exports and backups are not necessarily anonymous.

## Network requests and your choices

Every Internet recipient can see connection metadata such as your public IP address, requested URL/resource and normal HTTP information. HTTPS protects transport to the recipient; it does not conceal the request from that service. DNS resolvers, proxies and the OS can also process connection metadata. Provider requests use a product User-Agent, not a unique publisher device identifier.

The audited public API/download flows do not sign you into GitHub, Microsoft or Modrinth and do not send your Minecraft password. RCON authentication is a separate local-server operation. Some requests occur when you open a view: **MineDock 0.5.0 has no universal offline switch or per-feature network opt-out.** Avoid the relevant online feature or restrict application network access through the OS where required. A cache can reduce requests without guaranteeing none.

## Telemetry, logs and error reports

The audited MineDock code has no publisher analytics SDK, automatic crash-report upload, advertising identifier, remote persistent device identifier or publisher-operated telemetry endpoint. Its performance screen measures local server processes and stores local metrics. Random server/operation/session IDs are local database keys, not transmitted device identifiers.

The maintainer receives information you deliberately email or post in a GitHub issue/report. Issues are public and reveal your GitHub account and submitted content. Minimize and review logs before sharing: native server/plugin files and backups may still contain secrets or player data despite MineDock's best-effort redaction. Third-party services can keep their own access/download statistics.

## Retention, deletion and uninstalling

You control local storage. Removing a server first retains a recovery copy in app-owned trash. Independent backups, imported external originals, inventory safety copies, configuration/database copies and incremental objects can survive changes. Removing one record does not erase all other copies; no universal copy garbage collector is implemented.

- Backup retention is operator-configured and previewed before deletion.
- The application's maintenance path removes local process metrics older than seven days.
- Player detail retrieval prunes ended sessions older than 180 days and limits ended sessions to 20,000 per server; administration records are capped at 20,000 per server. These rules do not erase original Minecraft logs, player files, notes or backups.
- Application logs rotate around 5 MiB with a previous `.1` file. The in-memory console buffer holds up to 5,000 lines per server. Native server/plugin log rules are separate.
- Other local records, caches and backups can persist until deliberately removed. Cache expiry can cause a refresh without immediately deleting cached bytes.

On Windows, uninstall a Setup build through Installed apps. For Portable, close MineDock and remove its executable. The Windows uninstaller is configured to retain application data. Deleting the program does not delete your SQLite database, worlds or chosen storage folders; other OS package removal also does not automatically remove chosen storage.

To remove all local data, stop servers, close MineDock, review independent backups/external originals, then deliberately remove the actual profile and chosen storage/recovery locations you intend to erase. Logical deletion is not certified secure erasure; filesystem, OS and cloud backups can retain copies. Art-cache purge removes app-owned resources/registrations without deleting your original installed Minecraft client. Provider records have separate deletion controls.

## Security measures and limits

MineDock validates HTTPS download hosts and redirects, bounds responses/archive sizes, compares expected hashes and verifies updater metadata using a pinned Ed25519 key. Renderer sandboxing/context isolation, typed IPC, path checks and guarded inventory writes with verified backups reduce exposure. These controls do not guarantee error-free software or safe third-party programs.

Stored RCON secrets use Electron OS protection where available, with a local AES-256-GCM key fallback. The complete SQLite database and world files are not encrypted by MineDock. Native server configuration requires a plaintext RCON password, and native loopback RCON is not TLS. Protect your OS account, filesystem and backup locations.

Known RCON properties are excluded in relevant backup/export paths, but other secrets and player data can remain. Signed updater metadata is an integrity control separate from operating-system signing: current Windows/macOS downloads are unsigned. See [Security](security.md), [export privacy](package-format.md) and [Code signing policy](code-signing-policy.md).

## Contact and your rights

Use **[minedockapp@gmail.com](mailto:minedockapp@gmail.com)** for privacy questions, corrections and private requests. Gmail/Google and your mail provider process sender addresses, message contents/attachments and delivery metadata. Use GitHub issues for public bug reports, not private rights requests.

**Outstanding maintainer review:** the mailbox's actual access arrangements, applicable controller roles/legal bases and deletion practice have not been confirmed. Proposed handling is support/corrections and privacy requests without marketing reuse; keep correspondence only as needed to resolve the request and any applicable obligation/dispute, then delete or anonymize unnecessary content. Proposed bases, where applicable, are legitimate interests for proportionate support/corrections and legal obligations for statutory requests. These proposals are not presented as verified operational practices or blanket legal conclusions. Removing hosting infrastructure does not resolve these desktop/support issues.

Where applicable, rights can include access, correction, erasure, restriction, portability and objection, subject to their legal conditions. Contact your server operator about local player records, the maintainer about information sent directly, and each provider about its own processing. The maintainer cannot erase another person's local server or GitHub's infrastructure records. The [CNIL explains these rights](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/mes-demarches/les-droits-pour-maitriser-vos-donnees-personnelles); complaints can be directed to the [CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/adresser-une-plainte) or your competent supervisory authority.
